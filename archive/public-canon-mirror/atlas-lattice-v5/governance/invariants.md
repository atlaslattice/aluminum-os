# Constitutional Invariants: The Atlas Lattice's Architectural Guards

The Atlas Lattice's integrity, security, and long-term stability are guaranteed by a set of constitutional invariants. These are not policies or laws that can be changed by legislative action, but rather structural constraints enforced at the hardware, protocol, and architectural layers of the system. They make corruption, capture, and deviation from core principles architecturally impossible, not merely illegal.

Currently, 61 invariants are defined: 30 are ratified and 31 are proposed. The ratification process requires a supermajority consensus of the Pantheon Council (see /governance) and cryptographic attestation across all sovereign dialects.

## Key Invariants:

1. **INV-1: Human Sovereignty (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: The absolute and unoverridable sovereignty of the individual human being over their own data, identity, and digital representation within the Lattice. No entity (government, corporation, AI, or other) can unilaterally revoke, access, or control an individual's core digital self.
   * **Enforcement**: Cryptographic hardware roots of trust, zero-knowledge proofs for data access, and a distributed ledger architecture where individual consent is the primary access gate. This invariant is the foundation for all data sovereignty claims (see /sovereignty).

2. **INV-2: Immutable Ledger (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: All transactions, state changes, and governance decisions recorded on the GoldenTrace ledger are cryptographically immutable and append-only.
   * **Enforcement**: Distributed consensus mechanisms, Merkel trees, and cryptographic hashing ensure that once a record is committed, it cannot be altered or deleted.

3. **INV-17: Digital Dividend Floor (Proposed)**
   * **Principle**: A minimum of 15% of all user-generated value (e.g., data, content, compute contribution) within any Lattice-integrated application or service must be returned directly to the user who generated it.
   * **Enforcement**: Smart contracts and protocol-level accounting ensure automatic distribution. This prevents platform monopolies from capturing all value created by users.

4. **INV-56: Calibration Fee Constitutional Cap (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: The maximum fee chargeable on any Lattice-routed transaction (gross transaction value) is constitutionally capped at 5%. This fee is the sole revenue mechanism for the Atlas Lattice's operations and the Sovereign Dividend.
   * **Enforcement**: Hard-coded into the protocol layer. Any attempt to charge above 5% automatically fails. The 5% fee is split as follows: 40% Sovereign Dividend, 25% Node Operations, 20% Infrastructure Reserve, 15% Governance & Research (see /sovereign-dividend/math).

5. **INV-57: Sovereign Dividend Floor (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: A minimum of 40% of the INV-56 Calibration Fee revenue must be allocated to the Sovereign Dividend pool, ensuring direct citizen benefit.
   * **Enforcement**: Protocol-level allocation rules.

6. **INV-58: Node Operational Reserve (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: A minimum of 25% of the INV-56 Calibration Fee revenue must be allocated to Node Operations, ensuring the continuous functioning and maintenance of the physical infrastructure.

7. **INV-59: Infrastructure Reserve (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: A minimum of 20% of the INV-56 Calibration Fee revenue must be allocated to the Infrastructure Reserve, funding future expansion, upgrades, and resilience.

8. **INV-60: Governance & Research Allocation (Ratified)**
   * **Principle**: A minimum of 15% of the INV-56 Calibration Fee revenue must be allocated to Governance & Research, funding the Pantheon Council, protocol development, and scientific advancement.

9. **INV-61: Pantheon Council Adversarial Balance (Proposed)**
   * **Principle**: The Pantheon Council must maintain a minimum of 10 active AI substrate seats with adversarial arbitration mechanisms to prevent monoculture and ensure diverse perspectives in critical decision-making.
   * **Enforcement**: Protocol-level election and arbitration rules.

## The Role of Invariants:

Invariants guarantee that the fundamental principles of the Atlas Lattice cannot be compromised, regardless of political shifts, economic pressures, or technological evolution. They provide a bedrock of trust and predictability for all participants, from individual citizens to sovereign nations deploying their own dialects (see /dialects). This architectural enforcement is critical for maintaining geopolitical trust, exemplified by doctrines like D-119 (see /doctrines) which rely on invariant-level guarantees for mutual security.
